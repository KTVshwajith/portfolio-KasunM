Doctoral research

Video blockchain: footage that proves it hasn't been changed

My PhD at Auckland University of Technology (2020–2024) developed computational methods for storing surveillance and dash-cam video so that any edit, deletion or AI-generated insertion can be detected and located. It combines cryptographic hashing, a block-matrix data structure and deep-learning detectors.

Step 1

Split each frame into blocks

Every frame becomes a matrix of small pixel blocks, compressed and stored as binary. Small blocks let the system say where a change happened, not just that one did.

Step 2

Hash and chain them

Each block gets a SHA-256 fingerprint, and each frame's record includes the previous frame's hash. Altering any frame breaks every link after it.

Step 3

Flag AI-generated content

Locality-sensitive hashing (super-bit LSH) groups visually similar blocks, so the framework can recognise AI-generated and deepfake video, not only bit-level edits.

Applied computer vision

Posture analysis from one ordinary camera

With Mahesh Babu Purushothaman at AUT, I built a smart vision-based method that tracks 33 body landmarks and flags manual-lifting postures linked to musculoskeletal injury in construction work. This demo uses the same landmark model (BlazePose, via MediaPipe) to measure joint angles.
